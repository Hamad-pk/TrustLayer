# Design Decisions

Format: decision, rationale, trade-off. "Supersedes" marks changes from earlier drafts.

| ID | Decision | Rationale | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | Native ETH only | Removes token-behaviour risk (fee-on-transfer, no-return, blacklist) | ERC-20 deferred |
| D-02 | One non-upgradeable contract, many agreements | Simple deployment, discovery and testing | Shared ETH balance: isolation is bookkeeping only. Mitigated by per-agreement ledgers, no cross-agreement writes, and invariants over the sum of all agreements |
| D-03 | Sequential milestones, 1 to 20 per agreement | One active milestone means one live decision and bounded exposure | No parallel work. The cap of 20 bounds view and creation cost |
| D-04 | Terms immutable from creation. The terms hash is computed on-chain with `abi.encode` over (chainid, contract, agreementId, participants, arbiters, milestone amounts, deliverable hashes, criteria hashes, policy version). Every acceptance call takes the expected hash as an argument and reverts on mismatch | Closes the accept/edit race. Forces the acceptor to have independently computed the terms. `abi.encode` avoids `encodePacked` ambiguity with dynamic data | Mistakes require cancel and recreate |
| D-05 | Deliverable and acceptance-criteria hashes are committed per milestone | Gives arbiters an agreed anchor | Hash is useless without the preimage. UI must make the document available and the freelancer must obtain it before accepting |
| D-06 | Fixed constants: submission 14d, review 7d, revision response 14d, primary arbiter 14d, fallback arbiter 14d. Supersedes the 7d revision window | No per-agreement duration validation or extreme-duration risk. Revision window lengthened because missing it forfeits the milestone (D-13) | One-size-fits-all windows |
| D-07 | Both arbiters accept on-chain. The four addresses are nonzero and pairwise distinct | Records opt-in. Prevents role overlap and arbiter self-dealing by address reuse | Acceptance proves willingness, not future availability. Different-address collusion is undetectable |
| D-08 | No owner, admin, pause, sweep or override | Removes privileged-role abuse | No emergency recovery |
| D-09 | Pull payments. Credit is stored in the agreement struct as `clientCredit` and `freelancerCredit`. Withdrawal is `withdraw(agreementId, to)`: only the credited party may call, and it chooses `to` | Only two beneficiaries exist per agreement, so no mapping is needed. `to` lets a party whose address rejects ETH redirect (supersedes "retry with another account", which was impossible) | Frontend must never fill `to` silently. A lost key means lost credit |
| D-10 | Client may approve a `Submitted` milestone at any time until a dispute opens, even after the review deadline. The freelancer may not submit after the deadline | Late approval is the client waiving their own right. A late submission would let the freelancer waive the client's right | Asymmetry is deliberate. A freelancer who opens a dispute right after the deadline pre-empts a late approval |
| D-11 | Only the parties may open a dispute: client when `Submitted` (any time); freelancer when `Submitted` after the review deadline, or when `RevisionRequested` (any time). Timeouts and the default split stay permissionless. Supersedes "any account may open" | A third party adds nothing a stakeholder lacks the incentive to do, and could force 28 days of arbitration on two people who did not want it. Timeouts are deterministic, so a third-party caller can add liveness but cannot choose an outcome | If both parties are silent, nobody can force progress |
| D-12 | No revision-request limit. Supersedes "max 2" | Both parties hold unilateral escalation (D-11), so a revision loop only continues while both cooperate. Removes a counter and a rule | Cooperative loops are unbounded by design |
| D-13 | Freelancer silence loses the current milestone and all future ones to the client (refund, agreement Cancelled), in both `Working` and `RevisionRequested`. Supersedes the 50/50 timeout | In `Working` there is no on-chain evidence of any delivery, so paying 50% rewards doing nothing. In `RevisionRequested` the freelancer has an immediate dispute right and 14 days, so silence is a choice | Harsh for a freelancer who is genuinely unreachable for 14 days. Documented limitation |
| D-14 | If both arbiter windows lapse, any account may trigger a split: client `amount / 2` (floor), freelancer the remainder | Arbiter-failure fallback, not a reward for party silence. Both arbiters opted in, so reaching it requires two opted-in failures. Odd wei goes to the freelancer: a deterministic rule where 1 wei is immaterial | Can be unfair in a specific case. Parties accept it in advance |
| D-15 | `resolve(agreementId, freelancerAmount)`: the arbiter supplies one number (at most the milestone amount). The client gets the rest | The sum cannot be mis-specified. Arbiters have no credit slot, so they cannot pay themselves | Collusion remains possible |
| D-16 | Client may call `endAfterCurrent` at any time while the agreement is `Active`. All future (non-current) milestone funds are refunded to the client immediately. The current milestone proceeds normally, including any open dispute. The agreement completes after the current milestone settles. No kill fee | Gives the client a unilateral exit, so collusion between freelancer and arbiter costs at most one milestone per ruling | The freelancer is guaranteed only the current milestone, not future work |
| D-17 | Mutual cancellation removed from the MVP (deferred). Supersedes the proposal, consent, nonce and version design | `endAfterCurrent` + disputes + the freelancer's ability to stop cover the real exits. Removes a large state and test surface for a rare flow | Parties who agree on a custom split must go through a dispute |
| D-18 | Before funding, the client, freelancer, or either arbiter may call `cancelBeforeFunding`. This doubles as an arbiter or freelancer decline | No ETH is at risk. One mechanism for every pre-funding exit | An arbiter can veto before funding. That is what declining means |
| D-19 | Completion is automatic in the transaction that settles the last effective milestone | No finalization function, no privileged finalizer | None |
| D-20 | Only an aggregate `locked` amount per agreement is stored. Refund of future milestones is `locked - currentMilestoneAmount`. No loops anywhere in a timeout or exit | Timeouts must always fit in a block | Per-milestone outcomes are not stored: events carry them (D-23) |
| D-21 | Accounting law: `funded = locked + allocated` in every state, including terminal ones. Cancellation and exit move `locked` into `allocated` | One equation with no exceptions, so no case analysis in invariant handlers | None |
| D-22 | Contract logic never reads `address(this).balance`. Forced ETH is stranded forever and no one can sweep it | Balance comparisons with `==` are breakable by forced ETH. A sweep needs an admin (D-08) | Stranded ETH is lost. Tests track it with a ghost variable and assert an exact balance identity |
| D-23 | Per-milestone outcomes live in events (`MilestoneSettled` with amounts and reason), not storage. Milestone amounts are stored; deliverable and criteria hashes are emitted at creation and committed in the terms hash | Saves gas | Hashes are read from logs and verified against `termsHash`, not read directly from storage |
| D-24 | First transaction mined wins any race. State checks re-run at execution | No ordering assumptions | Front-running between parties exists (see D-10) |
| D-25 | Boundary convention: a normal action requires `block.timestamp < deadline`. A timeout or escalation requires `block.timestamp >= deadline`. Restored | Exactly one of the two paths is valid at any instant | Timestamps are coarse (about 15 seconds of validator drift) |

## Deferred

ERC-20 and SafeERC20, appeals and more arbiters, mutual cancellation with a negotiated split, partial approval, kill fees, per-agreement durations, on-chain evidence storage, reputation, signature-authorized withdrawal recipients, event indexing.

## Explicitly out of scope

Upgradeability, governance, tokenomics, protocol fees.
